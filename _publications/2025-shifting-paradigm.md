This article examines the emerging paradigm shift in consciousness studies from brain-bound materialist models toward quantum-based and field-theoretical frameworks. Using Paul C. Mocombe’s "Consciousness Field Theory" (CFT)—which posits consciousness as a fifth force of nature composed of quantum "psychions"—as a central case study, the work situates contemporary theories like Orch-OR and cemi field models within broader debates about non-local awareness, near-death experiences, and terminal lucidity. While acknowledging the scientific potential of these approaches to account for anomalous phenomena, the article applies a critical sociological lens to interrogate their underlying ideological functions, particularly the tendency to reassert human exceptionalism and hierarchical distinctions between human and non-human consciousness. The piece advocates for a "fearless but rigorous openness" in consciousness research—one willing to engage post-materialist hypotheses while remaining alert to how scientific innovation can embed class-bound narratives of unequal worth. This work connects to broader research interests in the sociology of mysticism, the social construction of consciousness, and the intersection of spirituality and scientific discourse.
